Research

I study how people use conversational AI in situations where simple instructions are not enough, and how models respond when those situations create competing demands. My work sits between AI and human behaviour, with a particular interest in what happens when the way a model is meant to behave differs from how it actually responds.

Therapeutic priors in frontier language models

This pilot study looks at how frontier language models behave in therapeutic-style conversations when their instructions conflict with their existing tendencies. The experiment keeps the system instruction fixed while gradually increasing pressure from the user. It asks when a model starts giving advice despite being told not to, and whether models with stronger tendencies to give advice are more likely to break the instruction.

I am also exploring whether chain-of-thought and mechanistic interpretability can help explain why these behaviours occur. The longer-term goal is to develop an experiment that can be repeated across different models as they change.

OTR Copilot

OTR Copilot was my Affective Computing capstone, an AI tool built for peer mentors at Over The Rainbow. It explored where AI could support an existing mentoring relationship without becoming part of the relationship itself.

The work was presented at the UCL Behaviour Change Conference and has since been accepted as a poster at AMIA 2026.

Multilingual conversational AI and migrant worker financial literacy

This participatory research project with migrant workers in Singapore looked at how language affects financial decision-making when people use conversational AI. Rather than focusing on what these tools could do in theory, it looked at how migrant workers actually used them and where they ran into problems when moving between languages.

The project was conducted through the Reach Alliance and is documented in the Reach Alliance case study.
