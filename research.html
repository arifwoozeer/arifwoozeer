Research

Most of my research is about AI and human behaviour, particularly what happens when people use conversational systems in situations that are more complicated than a standard prompt.

Therapeutic priors in frontier language models

This is an in-progress pilot project looking at what frontier language models tend to do in therapeutic-style conversations, especially when those tendencies conflict with an explicit instruction.

The experiment keeps the system instruction fixed while gradually increasing pressure from the user. If a model is told to listen without giving advice, for example, when does it start giving advice anyway? Do models with stronger tendencies in that direction break the instruction sooner?

There is also a second question behind the experiment: what accounts for the behaviour in the first place? Chain-of-thought and mechanistic interpretability are being explored as ways to look more closely at what happens when a model moves away from its instruction.

The aim is to turn the experiment into something that can be repeated as models change.

OTR Copilot

OTR Copilot was an AI tool for peer mentors at Over The Rainbow, developed as my Affective Computing capstone. The work was presented at the UCL Behaviour Change Conference and accepted as a poster at AMIA 2026.

The starting point was a simple question: if a mentor already has a relationship with someone, where can AI be useful without becoming part of the relationship itself?

Multilingual conversational AI and migrant worker financial literacy

This was participatory research with migrant workers in Singapore on how language affects financial decision-making when using conversational AI. The research focused on how migrant workers actually used these tools and the problems they ran into across languages.

The project was conducted through the Reach Alliance and is documented in their case study.
